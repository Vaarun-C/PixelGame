# Decision Log

What was settled in each round, in order. The details live in the topic files; this is the record of when. (Rounds 1–7 were logged only inside the topic files; rounds 32–33 settled nothing new, their options were decided in round 34.)

**Settled in round 8:** deceived consent = forced; one meter; freeing at wayside shrines; all gods old, the dark ones hid while weak; Crimson speaking; fusing; shards not soul; persistence answers hidden monks.

**Settled in round 9:** uttering burns the soul (only unspoken breaks loose souls); forced runes have downsides, willing none; only offering is the dark gods' meal (uttering feeds them only indirectly, as wrongdoing; refined round 13); dark monks hide because the trap needs trust; failed fuses add corruption; compatibility known after reading both; the Whisper (75–99%) and the Pull (100%); the Wanderer as former war chaplain; start and respawn in a village.

**Settled in round 10:** found shrines persist through the Whisper; withdrawal outweighs the Pull; the Wanderer's names return only through reading; dialogue lore rules; iron wire (from the dead man's mail; iron is the jailer's metal); who sends him back depends on corruption; the dead rise from souls loosed by stones broken in the war; uttering still burns the soul, and its hatred raises a Hollow scaled to the spell; no deliberate smashing; offering earns the dark gods' favour.

**Settled in round 11:** corruption follows acts; the Wanderer starts at about 25–35%; uttering spends the name itself, so a hatred Hollow has no name left.

**Settled in round 12:** the favour is B (dark monks know an offerer) and C (the stones go quiet), scaling with the tier offered; its hints are subtle at low corruption and plain near madness; the offering act (spent reliquary below 75%, anywhere from the Whisper, through a known dark monk; glow sinks, stone greys, wire falls loose; more corruption than an utterance, no shards, scaled by tier; no Hollow rises); spent vs forgotten names. F is held in reserve for the Crimson prisoners. Death and return stay as in round 10.

**Settled in round 13:** at the Pull, both uttering and offering ease withdrawal, offering more (option iii), because the dark gods consume an offered soul but cannot consume a burned one; uttering feeds them only indirectly, as wrongdoing, never as a meal. Quiet stones: only runes up to the offered tier; Crimson never quiets. Favour text is always true, even in the Whisper. Loose souls: binding calls a name, not a body (names from kin, rarely a rite cord; the result tells where the soul is). Hatred Hollows: nameless, only fought, stay dead, ember tells, no body, strength by the corpse seized.

**Settled in round 14:** fusing willing runes (willing only if both are willing and consent; refusal or any forced partner gives a forced stone); a fuse that holds adds corruption only if the result is forced.

**Settled in round 15:** compatibility vs consent as three gates (type, compatibility, consent) with separate messages; incompatibility is a fact about the souls (killer and killed cannot share a stone); a refused fuse is wired by the Wanderer with iron scrap. Loose souls B1 + B4: nearest unburned corpse, killing only lays a Hollow down, it walks again after a lull in an unrisen corpse, killing adds no corruption, a risen corpse carries only what it still had, burned bodies do not walk. Dark monk lies C1 (scripted swap, caught by reading and ledger, to the clean only). Clue names never lie, even in the Whisper.

**Settled in round 16:** the Wanderer served out of duty, not understanding, and the story is his slow understanding of runes and his rites; runes became a currency once the kingdoms learned to wield them, and the trade drove forced trapping for profit, feeding corruption and the dark gods; the name "the Greying" (meaning open).

**Settled in round 17:** the relic doctrine; willing runes made only by those who knew and chose stone (the Crimson circle); the Greying is G1, Forgetting; pantheon shape P1 "Shadows" with Wend, Ennick, Hush and Mortmain. K1–K4 rejected.

**Settled in round 18:** the pantheon in full: three pairs (Ennick/Hush, Gled/Grudge, Wend/Mortmain); the good death in three parts (Naming, Letting Go, Walking), each dark god hidden in one part; Gled, the Pyre (T1) replaces the Banked Fire; Mortmain the Keeper taught the holding, Hush gave the silence (reading; replaced round 36); madness stays unowned. T2–T4 and Sooth rejected.

**Settled in round 19:** Grudge's favour "Not yet"; all three dark rites consume the soul (Mortmain locks, Hush eats the name, Grudge twists into a Hollow) and all ease the Pull, uttering less; only Wend's rite lowers corruption, Ennick and Gled free the soul onto Wend's road for small practical buffs; Mortmain is the permanent one; the false rite withholds the release, not the name; kinder dark-rite words in the Whisper; a known dark monk performs any dark rite for metal; the name comes first; war cords tied by chaplains.

**Settled in round 20:** the needs model for choosing rites (each soul lacks parts of the good death, finished in order, ending at Wend's road; dark rites are the cheap kindness); places and words kept; four layers of learning a soul's need; procedural common needs, authored notable runes; dark rites learned by being fooled once; the Pull (dark-only prompts, kneel through withdrawal for the good words, echoes stop, remembered shrines stand); the Whisper twists wishes, never names; every dark rite costs corruption; the Grudge rite casts no spell. The "good/dark switch per place" framing rejected.

**Settled in round 21:** favour owners (Hush: quiet stones; Mortmain: dark monks know him; Grudge: "Not yet"); Ennick's and Gled's small practical buffs; Wend sends the Wanderer back at low corruption; the living can be bound without consent only through a dark rite of its own (the victim goes hollow, heaviest corruption), which releases C2; common souls have one need plus the release, notable souls can stack.

**Settled in round 22:** currency N2 (no coin passes by count; old coins pass only by weight, most of them cut: bronze crows, silver marks, gold heads); why coin stopped (R1 no mint, R2 debasement, R3 no common trust); wire money (iron-cored marks only, so every coin is nicked); where the old coins went (hoards, cut change, melted to bars and rings, scrapped marks, with the dead, up into fallen vaults); iron refused as money for a glut and for wire money, not as the jailer's metal, which only the Wanderer learns (changes round 10's reason); the barterer's iron line as proposed text.

**Settled in round 23:** why metal is worth anything (it keeps when food doesn't; the habit outlived the kings); grave coin, the kin's gift for Wend's road, which Wend never asks for; the rite fee, so a death costs twice; village monks live on the fee and have no families; cheap monks let the dark monks in, but price is no tell; the war chaplains' free rite was the false one; robbing grave coin is text only. Bronze as tools, silver against Hollows, trade beyond the map and married lay brothers rejected.

**Settled in round 24:** grey huts are the asset palette, not lore; G2 the Vault Night rejected; coin faces crisp but unremembered; a whole uncut coin is a text-only find. Death: at low corruption the road carries only the man (gear and sockets return, the bag stays where he fell); dying again before reaching it, a Hollow carries the bag off and must be killed (B; A rejected); from the Whisper the bag comes back but the dark gods take one rune per death, at random from bag and sockets together, consumed as an offering with no corruption since he did not choose it; they take only what is on his person (a field bag is safe, sockets are not); only loose-soul Hollows pick up a dropped bag, and with none nearby it stays where it lies; "Not yet" spares these costs.

**Settled in round 25:** reading vs the relic doctrine: the doctrine admits the echo's content and denies presence (the ring and the track; "A man would answer. A stone repeats."); forced echoes fixed and nameless for every reader; a name returns only to the man who spoke it at the rite; the chaplains' rule "A shriving hand holds no stone"; willing stones speak their name plainly to any reader, and those who noticed became the willing/Crimson circle; the Wanderer's break is staged (first reading a crack: Pale Rend, "He said my name. Then nothing." / "You know this name. You said it."; mid-arc an echo changes, "An echo does not answer"; more stages allowed); UI label "Echo:" becomes the man's name. Stone-sickness: the order's cover for killing men who asked the right questions, the one exception to "dark monks don't murder" (only to keep the secret, never to fill stones; deaths must look natural).

**Settled in round 26:** how the war ended: wire money broke pay (desertion, starvation, unfarmed fields); stones smashed by the thousand, the dead rising faster than they could be buried; the woken things sealed in Crimson by the willing circle. The crowns sacking the chapels was not chosen; the crowns' fate stays open.

**Settled in round 27:** the cause of the war is the testament: a king given the false rite at home by a court keeper; his echo "Not him." certified by the order as his will; heirs and neighbours at war over its meaning; the readings of the king as the first atrocity; the stone offered to Hush late in the war (by whom, withheld), so no peace was possible; "him" was the chaplain, revealed through witnesses (a servant's or guard's echo, a scraped court-chapel ledger page); hunger as the background; the chaplain was not the Wanderer; game traces as design canon. The crowns: several, small, unnamed, shifting, no victor; a generation-long war; the crowns forgotten, not destroyed (the Greying).

**Settled in round 28:** who in the order knew: the layers (the abbot, the Mother House brothers, village monks, war chaplains, gleaners) and the keepers, few and hidden at every level, the only ones who knew; the chain chaplain → gleaner → cart → reliquary → Mother House ledger → crown; whether the abbot knew is kept for a late reveal; the keepers have no leader and serve the dark gods directly; a few old keepers are deeply corrupt, given boons, mad, and lead or brainwash recruits. They hide in remote places (the village dark monks are the young, lucid ones); their boons are the three favours deepened (an unremembered face, known by every keeper, "Not yet" again and again); they live at the Pull, which is why they ran the trade, recruit and want his stones. Killing a living person adds corruption. The Mother House is fallen, abandoned and forgotten, found through ledger clues, with old keepers in its undercroft: the late-game place. The old keepers are late-game bosses (utterances raising hatred Hollows; stones drop as souls to free; they tempt but never deal for a god). Killing the living: keeper bosses cost corruption, earned back by freeing their stones; self-defence counts; the blade can't be swung at villagers, and living enemies appear only where the story puts them; a flat cost, more than an utterance, about a dark rite, below the living binding.

**Settled in round 29:** the face on the gold heads is the testament king's; his kingdom struck the heads, and the war spread them everywhere. The Chapel Ruins fell by CR1, the keepers clearing the larder ("resting" by night, the lie to the gleaners, a wall of the dead, the keepers leaving with the sale book and the best stones, deserters the next day); they left the rite ledgers to be guarded by the dead; the prior wrote the last line; chapels without keepers fell other ways. One old keeper stayed sealed in one hand-made chapel: the first boss, who starts revealing the truth: weak and starved from ten years at the Pull; the Wanderer must kill him, and the fight teaches the loop (corruption up, free his stones, corruption down); he raves the truth and can be written off as stone-sick; his face is unremembered; he carries a sale-book page, the first pointer to the Mother House. Shards come only from spoken stones (the war left them wherever it was fought; gleaners gathered them, chapels bought them as carving stock); a stone broken unspoken crumbles to dull grit.

**Settled in round 30:** the Crimson prisoners are the Unfallen (men Grudge would not let die; a willing jailer gave the yes; names torn across ledgers; buff and cost are his strength and burden; the king's champion as example). A Crimson rune can be socketed, uttered (huge spell, prisoner out, jailer consumed, a lot of corruption), never offered (no god accepts it), or released (the jailer freed, the prisoner fought). **Settled in round 36:** using stones leaked (rivals read a few seized stones) but making stayed secret, so every crown bought once the keepers sold; the secret is the keeper-made blank, and both the blank and the withholding rite are Mortmain's craft; Hush only consumes what is offered and gives the silence and forgetting, never holds souls; without a blank a cut-short rite leaves the soul loose; war chaplains were handed blanks dressed as keepsakes or prayer stones for the dying to grip; the Paymaster bought blanks from the keepers; the Crimson Circle needed blanks (source open, then settled round 37).

**Settled in round 37:** rebuilding is patching: the carver fills a shattered rune's cracks with mortar, it can't be fully repaired (why it drops a tier), and the blank is still the stone underneath ("That's the most I can do."). What feeds each dark god: Mortmain eats a soul's freedom (trapping), Hush its name (offering), Grudge its refusal (uttering is a scrap of a meal, rewording round 13's "never a meal"; his pyre rite is his full meal; the hatred Hollow is his leftovers; the Unfallen are his living coals). Forced runes' downsides are Mortmain's grip; willing souls are outside it (no downside, they speak their own name); Crimson's downside comes from the prisoner. Forced echoes are nameless because Mortmain withholds the name (replaces "Hush's silence"; the round 36 Ennick proposal dropped). The Crimson Circle began as the king's inner circle of readers (some from the Order made the runes, others put the souls in); they learned the truth when he made runes from prisoners and sealed his strongest maddened guards, so the sealing began in the border wars; they knew the craft, which settles how they made stones and got blanks. Grudge proposals (keeps wars going; the Letting Go starves him; willing utterance raises no Hollow) and the Circle proposals (a) and (b) stay open.

**Settled in round 38:** the war's last ~10 years: after the crowns stopped buying, the keepers sold blanks straight to the captains to the war's end, because every stone fed the Quiet gods; stones became company pay; supply never ran out, and the Wanderer trapped souls until the last day ("blanks running short" rejected; the dead outran the living because stones were broken unspoken). The game opens with a playable prologue at the Pull, then cuts to year 0 in a village. The stone's echo as a later beat below the Pull stays a proposal.
