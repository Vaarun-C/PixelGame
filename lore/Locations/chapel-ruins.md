# Chapel Ruins

The old chapels where stones were kept. They fell late in the war when the stone market died: the keepers secretly fed the unsold stones to Hush, had the rest smashed so the dead would rise and guard the place, and slipped away with the sale book. Deserters got the blame.

## Canon

- **Fallen walls, pillars, statues, reliquaries, runestones.** Runes are far more common here than anywhere else, Ward most of all. (seen in: biomes.js, reliquary loot)
- **The reliquaries** are where the souls taken under the false rites were kept. **Spent reliquaries** are the old larder where offerings are made (round 12).
- **Chapel ledgers** (new prop, design canon) record names, including pieces of Crimson prisoners' true names. The ledger is Ennick's and Hush's rite place (round 19). Since round 47 every village has its own ledger too; chapel ledgers keep the war's names.

### How the chapels fell: the keepers cleared the larder (round 29, CR1)

- **The money stopped.** Late in the war (~10 years ago, TB) the crowns' silver ran out and no one bought stones. Gleaners kept bringing them; they piled up in the nave. Wounded and refugees filled the chapel; the unburned dead filled the crypt and yard.
- **The "resting".** For weeks the keepers offered stones to Hush at night: a short prayer, the lid closed on the light. They called it laying unsold relics to rest. Ordinary monks saw candles snuffed; under the doctrine a grey stone is a lamp run out. Kin far away forgot names, and no one notices forgetting. That is why reliquaries are "spent".
- **The lie to the gleaners.** When deserters were seen on the road, the keepers told the gleaners the last carts were enemy stones, cursed, to be broken. Captains had ordered the same on the field, and gleaners never read stones. They smashed stones all night in the yard.
- **The keepers' reason: a wall of the dead,** so no one would go in to count stones or read the ledgers.
- **The rising.** The loosed souls took the crypt and yard dead. The wounded died first, then the gleaners; the prior stayed and died; the other monks ran.
- **The keepers left in the night** with the sale book (who bought what, for how much) and the best stones, for the Mother House.
- **The deserters came the next day,** unpaid men after the chapel silver. Most died inside; a few got away with a cut-up chest.
- **Why the keepers didn't burn the ledgers:** they took the dangerous one, the sale book. The rite ledgers hold only names, innocent under the doctrine, and useful to keepers (binding needs a name), so the dead guard them. Smoke draws people; deserters breaking relics is just another war story.
- **The prior's slip.** The last line in the rite ledger, "Taint on the last carts. Broken by order." (proposed exact text), was written by the prior, an honest monk, before he died. The keepers don't know it exists.
- **Chapels without keepers fell in other ways** (deserters, or loose dead from nearby fields).
- **The [first keeper](../Characters/the-first-keeper.md)** is sealed in one hand-made chapel here.

- **Survivors:** people believe the deserters broke the reliquaries and woke the dead; the lie feeds distrust of soldiers and strangers. A survivor who was a child remembers the stones broken the night *before*. Peasant: "They broke stones in the yard all night. In the morning the yard got up." Village monk: "Deserters did it. Everyone says so."

Broken by order."; hammers and stone grit in the yard; gleaners' bodies with pliers; deserters' bodies with cut silver; grey stones and loose wire in pried reliquaries ("Grey stones and loose wire.
