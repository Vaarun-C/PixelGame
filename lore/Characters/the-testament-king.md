# The Testament King

The king whose last words started the war. He secretly fed the dark gods and grew rich on stones of beasts, then fought his jealous neighbours with stones of men. Dying, he asked for the true rite, heard it cut short, and said "Not him." Everyone read it as a will about his two sons. It was about the monk at his bed.

## Canon

### His reign (round 34, option M-A; round 37)

- **The keepers came first.** They already hid inside the Order. The king knew the true rite (he heard it at his father's death) and knew of the Quiet gods; he spotted the keepers and, instead of exposing them, **became their patron.**
- **No pact.** He fed the dark gods through the keepers' rites and the boons followed. The gods never bargain. (rounds 18, 28)
- **His inner circle of readers** (round 37): **a few came from the Order and made the runes; the others put the souls inside** (the first tool runes, the beast stones). **The king knew of the Quiet gods, so he didn't question how it worked.** This inner circle became the [Crimson Circle](../Organizations/the-crimson-circle.md). (Round 34 had said "a trusted few were his readers"; see [The king's readers](../Organizations/the-kings-readers.md).)
- **Beast stones:** kind names are faint, so they are **Pale.** A beast stone lends its verb to what it is set in (a yoke, a plough, a churn), like a stone lashed to a blade; it works while set and while its reader lives. **The kingdom grew rich; the gold heads were struck then.** (round 34; round 34 says the keepers trapped the beasts, see Open)
- **The beasts were used up.** The real animals thinned, then what ate them starved; only the crows did well.
- **Border wars:** rivals were jealous of his prosperity (round 37). He set prisoners' stones in his champions' blades: **his kingdom was the first to wield men's stones.** Grudge's rite at the pyre gave him "Not yet" (it stops blows, not wasting); offerings to Hush kept his stones' downsides quiet.
- **When he began making runes from prisoners of war, his inner circle learned the truth** about the Quiet gods and the real rites, and turned the craft on the strongest of his guards. See [The Crimson Circle](../Organizations/the-crimson-circle.md). (round 37)
- **The craft stayed secret** (round 34, the user's correction): he never told anyone how it was done, and no one could work it out from outside. The neighbours did not copy him. **Using leaked, making didn't** (round 36): rivals read a few seized stones but couldn't make more.
- **The keepers left his dying court** for the Order's houses and began selling stones to everyone; that is how the other crowns got stones (round 35). **One stayed:** the [court keeper](the-court-keeper.md).

### His end (round 34)

- **Corruption wore him down, short of the Pull,** so he was sane at his deathbed.
- **He asked for the long rite,** wanting for himself what he had denied every soul. **His own court keeper gave the field form,** because the king was the richest stone in the realm. He heard the words stop and knew the man, because he served the same gods.

### The testament (round 27)

- **His echo was "Not him."** Under the relic doctrine an echo points where the foot was going, so **the Order certified it as his will.** His heirs were **his two sons** (round 38d); each swore it meant the other, and they split the kingdom and went to war. (seen in: prologue line 2, "His sons each swore it meant the other.") **Both sons died before the war ended; their claims passed to their sons, regents or captains, so the war outlived them** (round 38e). See [The crowns and heirs](../Organizations/the-crowns-and-heirs.md).
- **The readings of the king were the first atrocity.** Reading is personal, so every claimant needed his own sworn reader. His stone was Rend, so a champion socketed it and cut down prisoners before the lords until it read. Same words every time, and proof to each side. The first reader was [the king's champion](the-unfallen.md). (*Round 39 flag:* "socketed" kept on purpose until the user decides whether smithed settings existed as a rich man's fitting; elsewhere canon says "lashed". Same for "set prisoners' stones in his champions' blades" above.)
- **Late in the war someone offered his stone to Hush** (who is deliberately withheld). He was consumed and forgotten; the words survived, written, sewn and sworn. A fight over a sentence with no speaker could not be settled, so it ran on as a harvest until it ran out.
- **The truth: "him" was the chaplain.** The one dying man who saw through the rite said so, and was read as politics. To read him right you had to disbelieve the doctrine. **The king's chaplain was not the Wanderer.**
- **The truth comes out through witnesses:** a servant's or guard's stone echo (proposed: "He looked at the brother, not at his sons.") and a scraped page in a court-chapel ledger. **The king is consumed, so no need of his can be finished.**
- **His face is on the gold heads,** crisp and unknown (round 29). See [Currency](../Mechanics/currency-and-barter.md).
- **Game traces** (design canon, not built; texts proposed): faded banners whose description says the words were stitched there once; "Not him" cut into milestones on the old roads; a ruined [court chapel](../Locations/court-chapel.md) with a greyed Gold-sized stone, its wire hanging loose; ledgers tallying the readings (champions, prisoners cut); Mother House sale pages for certified readings. Villager line: "Two words, and we all died for one of them."

## Open

- **Between the border wars and his death** (round 34, the user's idea, not settled): after repeated use of spells and runes, the king and his soldiers withered; with no one left to read the runes, famine slowly killed the people; the readers learned of his corruption and became the Circle; the dark monks saw his ruin and escaped.
  - **Reading A (recommended): this all comes before the testament war.** Border wars → he and his uttering champions wither → readers see why → yokes go dead, famine → keepers leave and sell → he dies, "Not him" → the heirs' war. Keeps all canon and gives the hunger a cause. Round 35 and 37 answers only make sense under A.
  - **Reading B: it replaces the testament war.** Breaks "Not him", the readings, a generation of war, wire money, the chapel market, the Unfallen and TB's dates (rounds 26–31).
  - **"Repeated use" must mean uttering and dark rites, not lashing,** or the Wanderer would wither for wearing his blade.
  - **The famine, fitted to reading:** a beast stone works while its reader lives. To re-read an ox-stone a man must pull the plough himself; starving men can't, and the oxen were already in the stones. Like a village that sold its horses for one machine, then lost the only man who could run it. Fits TB if it starts ~35 years ago and runs through the war.
  - **Wording clash:** canon calls the hunger "blight and failed harvests"; this makes it dead yokes. One must go. (history review round 36, point 6)
- **Who trapped the beasts:** round 34 says the keepers; round 37 says the readers put the souls inside. Flagged; see [Blanks](../Runes/blanks.md).
- **Who held his stone** (history point 8). Proposal: it stayed in the court chapel in the Order's keeping as a "neutral relic"; each side paid the Mother House for a certified reading; it was offered there by Hush's ledger rite, so the scraped page and the greyed stone are one event.
- **His reign has no dates; how he first knew of the Quiet gods is not said.** (history point 12)
- **Withheld:** who offered his stone.
