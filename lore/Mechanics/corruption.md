# Corruption

The Wanderer has one meter: corruption. It rises with every soul he traps, burns or gives to the dark gods, and with killing the living. It falls only when he frees a soul at Wend's road. At 75% the world's text starts to lie (the Whisper); at 100% he goes mad (the Pull). Every step back down is walked, not bought.

## Canon (rounds 6–14, 20, 28, 38; design canon, not built)

- **One meter: corruption.** "Gratitude" is only the name for what lowers it. (round 8)
- **Corruption follows acts, not intent.** A soul trapped unknowing still counts. (round 11)
- **He starts the game at about 25–35%,** below the Whisper. (round 11; exact value is tuning)
- **Why he starts there** (round 38): thousands of trappings put him at the Pull by the war's end (~3 years ago). For three years he gave the long rite, without a blank, to the dying he met; each full good death counted as Wend's rite, and at the Pull each cost blood (the kneel). His corruption fell slowly to 25–35% by year 0. See [The Wanderer](../Characters/the-wanderer.md).
- **When he crossed** (round 38b): he was sane for most of his service; he crossed into the Whisper late and hit the Pull on or near the last field.
- **He barely survived the withdrawal** in those three years (round 38b); the prologue shows him starving at the Pull, and he arrives at year 0 thin and worn.
- **Backstory only** (round 38): in play, the long rite to the dying does NOT lower corruption. Only freeing at Wend's road does. **Why** (round 38b): the war gave him endless dying men; now few die near him, and villages have their own monks, who gave the rites to their ancestors, so they'd rather have them than a stranger.
- **It rises** when he utters a rune, traps a soul (binding), rebuilds a rune, speaks a Crimson rune, fails a fuse, makes a forced stone by fusing (round 14), offers a rune (more than an utterance, round 12), or does any dark rite (round 20). (rounds 6–9, 12, 14, 20)
- **Killing a living person adds corruption** (round 28; the user: "it's a horrid act"). Hollows are not living, so killing them is free (round 15).
  - **Keeper bosses cost corruption to kill;** freeing their stones earns it back.
  - **Self-defence still counts.**
  - **The blade can't be swung at villagers.** Living enemies appear only where the story puts them.
  - **The cost is flat** (a living man has no tier): more than an utterance, about a dark rite, below the living binding (the heaviest act).
- **It falls only through Wend's rite** (freeing at a wayside shrine), because only it finishes a good death. (round 20) Ennick's and Gled's steps give no drop.
- **It hurts on the way up,** more the closer it gets to 100%. (round 8)
- **The higher it gets, the more reasonable the dark gods seem.** (round 6)

### 75–99%: The Whisper (round 9)

- **The world's text starts to lie:** item descriptions, villager lines and prompts read wrong, and wayside shrines look like rubble. (The user chose "A and B": A is the band before madness, B madness itself.)
- **The dark rite words read kinder** (round 19): "Lay him in the niche" reads "Keep him safe."
- **It can twist what a soul wants, never its name** (round 20): "Tell her I'm sorry" may read "Keep me with her." Names stay true (Ennick).
- **A shrine he has already found stays where it was,** even if it looks like rubble. Memory beats the lie. (round 10)
- **Favour text is always true,** even in the Whisper. (round 13)

### 100%: madness, The Pull (round 9)

- **He goes mad** from worshipping gods too ancient to bear. Utterances grow stronger and no longer add corruption. In exchange, **withdrawal:** when not feeding, he loses health, dims and slows.
- **What eases withdrawal** (round 13, option iii; widened round 19): uttering and all three dark rites; **uttering eases it less** (it is only a scrap of a meal for Grudge, round 37).
- **The good words fade** (round 20):
  - At a rite place only the dark rite is offered.
  - **A good rite anyway:** kneel and hold the button while withdrawal drains health (screen dims, hands shake); after a few seconds the good words come back, faint. Atonement at the Pull costs blood.
  - **Echoes stop,** so new souls' needs can't be learned; souls already understood can still be finished.
  - Remembered shrines still stand.
- **Madness must hurt: withdrawal outweighs the stronger utterances.** Staying at 100% must never be a winning strategy. (round 10)
- **The way back:** walk to a wayside shrine and free souls; each freed soul pulls him back below 100%. (round 9)
- **The prologue is played at the Pull** (round 38): the mad chaplain on the field reads the Rend stone.

## Open

- **Harm below 75%.** Proposal: corruption sharpens every forced rune's downside (the souls resent a corrupt bearer). Round 37 framing: Mortmain's grip tightens on a corrupt bearer's stones.
- **Does the Whisper's lying text continue at 100%?** Proposal: yes, worse.
- **Withdrawal speed** is tuning; the lore rule is only that it must outweigh the Pull's stronger utterances.
