# Currency and Barter

There's no money anyone trusts by count any more. Old coins pass only by weight, most of them cut into pieces and nicked to check for an iron core. Metal is worth something because it keeps when food rots, and because every death costs a family twice: coin for the road and a fee for the monk.

## Canon

### Coin (round 22, N2; replaces "There are no coins")

- **No coin passes by count. Old coins pass only by weight, most of them cut.** Bronze **crows**, silver **marks**, gold **heads**. Metal is counted in grams. (seen in: Bronze/Silver/Gold descriptions, barter)
- **Barterers:** one per village. They trade metal by weight at rates that drift village to village and over time, keeping a cut. Base rate about 10 bronze to a silver, 10 silver to a gold. (seen in: barter, village.js)
- **Why coin stopped** (round 22):
  - **R1 no mint:** the crowns fell; no one strikes coin.
  - **R2 the war debased the coin:** crowns paying armies thinned the silver until a stamp said nothing about weight.
  - **R3 no common trust:** walled villages share no standard; a dead king's stamp is worth only its metal (fits drifting rates, built).
- **Wire money** (round 22): in the war's last years (~10 years ago, round 34) the crowns struck silver marks with an iron core under a silver wash. Only marks: bronze was too cheap to fake; gold heads stayed honest because they went to captains and ransoms. The wash wore through (~8 to 4 years ago) and people found they held iron. **So every barterer nicks every coin:** bright inside, it is weighed; grey inside, it goes in the scrap.
- **Where the old coins went** (round 22): good coin hoarded as the bad drove it out, its owners died, so it lies in chests, tombs and the ground; the rest was cut into halves, quarters and slivers for change, and nicked; tested pieces melted into bars and rings (why metal is counted in grams); iron-cored marks scrapped; much went with the dead (pay on uncleared bodies, pyre ash); the silver paid for stones went into war chests, crown vaults and chapel treasuries, now fallen. **A purse today is a few whole coins, many cut pieces and some lumps, all weighed.** (fits: items.js loot tables)
- **Wages stayed coin; stones were never pay** (round 38b; corrects round 38). Soldiers and chaplains drew coin wages to the end, which is why wire money breaking (~8 to 4 years ago) broke pay. The crowns bought *finished* stones with silver as weapons, war chests and ransoms until their silver ran out (~11 years ago); after that stones had no buyer. Blanks were bought by the crown or company and handed to chaplains with their wages, as tools. **The one exception is the Paymaster,** who paid his men in stones. See [Origin of runes](../Runes/origin-of-runes.md), [Blanks](../Runes/blanks.md).
- **Iron is refused as money** for a glut and for wire money; still used as scrap for nails and hinges. (fits: only bronze, silver and gold trade; scrap is never bartered, items.js, barter.js) See [Willing and forced](../Runes/willing-and-forced.md). Barterer line (proposed): "Iron's what they paid us in, and iron's what the fields are made of. Keep it."

### Why metal is worth anything (round 23)

- **Metal keeps when food doesn't.** Grain rots, rats eat it, it gets stolen; metal can be buried and saved for a bad winter. Everyone takes it because everyone else does; the habit outlived the kings.
- **Grave coin (road coin):** a crow on each eye of the dead, silver for a richer death, the kin's gift for the road. People keep the custom without knowing what runes are. **Wend never asks a fee;** the poor only fear she does, so they starve before they spend the coin.
- **The rite fee:** a proper last rite takes a monk's time (the naming, the cord, the pyre, the walk to the shrine), and he is paid in metal. A death costs a family twice, so people save metal all their lives. (See [The Order](../Organizations/the-order.md).)
- Robbing grave coin costs nothing in play; text only. Proposed pickup: "Eye-coin, half melted. Someone paid for his road."

### Coin faces (rounds 24, 29)

- **Crisp but unremembered.** A war in living memory can't wear them flat; no one knows whose they are (the Greying). Barterer (proposed): "Some king. Does it matter which?"
- **The gold heads carry the testament king's face.** His kingdom struck them; the war spread them through every crown's lands. He was offered to Hush, so the face is crisp and unknown. The crows and marks carry other kings, forgotten as their crowns were. The barterer's line is unknowingly about the man the war was fought over.
- **A whole uncut coin is a rare find,** text only. Proposed: "A whole head. Uncut. Someone hid this well."

## Open (proposals, none canon)

- **Stones were priced by glow:** anyone can see tier, type, notches and wire; reading doesn't pass with the stone; under the doctrine who was inside "didn't matter".
- **Thinning before the iron core** (round 21 draft): marks struck thinner, then copper under the wash.
- **What the barterer does:** beam scale, nicks every coin, cuts coin for change (hack-silver); drifting rates are his trust in his own scale.
- **Why no one strikes coin again:** R1 and R3, plus a stamp promises a weight no one trusts. A superstition that a struck face calls the dead, **only as a line, never as the cause.**
- **N3 village tallies** (notched sticks for regulars): held, little gain.
- **Proposed in-game text:** Bronze: "Old crows and broken fittings. Weighed, never counted." Silver: "Cut marks, mostly. Nicked to the core." Gold: "A head no one remembers. A little goes far." Loot: "A soldier's last pay, cut in quarters." Barterer: "I don't care whose face. I care what it weighs." / "Grey under the nick. Wire money. Take it back." / "The scale has no mouth." Icons: cut half and quarter coins.
- **Rune money, past and present:** see [Origin of runes](../Runes/origin-of-runes.md).

## Rejected

- **"There are no coins," literally (N1).** Replaced round 22 by N2.
- **K1–K4** (round 16), rejected round 17: **K1** coin as a receipt for stones (coin is worth its metal; explains nothing a fallen mint doesn't); **K2** a stamp is a name, so no one strikes coin (a taboo wouldn't spread evenly, and would forbid all marks); **K3** no crown left, alone (thin; kept inside R1); **K4** the scale has no mouth (a slogan; usable as a line).
- **Round 23 options:** bronze valued for tools (says nothing about silver or gold); silver wards off Hollows (stock trope, makes silver a weapon); trade with a richer land beyond the map (opens a needless question); married lay-brother monks (monks keep only themselves).
- **Coin faces worn past knowing.** Rejected round 24.
- **Two layers of money / stones as company pay** (open proposal, then round 38 "stones became company pay"). Replaced round 38b: wages stayed coin, or wire money could not have broken pay. Kept from it: the crowns bought finished stones with silver (war chests, ransoms, weapons), which is how Mortmain's market drank the silver.
