# Willing and Forced Runes

Almost every rune holds a soul that was tricked or forced into it. Those runes are wrapped in iron wire and carry a downside, which is Mortmain's grip on the soul. A few souls walked in by choice: those stones are bare, have no downside, and say their own name.

## Canon

### Forced (rounds 6, 8, 9; design canon, not built)

- **A soul trapped by force or by deceit gives a rune with an upside and a downside.** Every forced rune has a downside. Souls taken under the false rites are forced, so nearly every rune found is forced.
- **The downside is Mortmain's grip** (round 37). A forced soul is held by Mortmain, who limits what it can do; that limit is the downside.
- **The downside scales with rarity:** higher tier, heavier downside. (round 8)
- **High tiers stay worth lashing:** the upside must outgrow the downside, so Gold is a better deal than Pale, not a trap. (round 9)
- *Gap:* today only Crimson runes have downsides in game.

### Willing (rounds 6, 8, 9, 17)

- **A soul that came into a rune willingly.** Rare, precious, **no downside.** (round 9 settles that "every rune has a downside" meant forced runes only)
- **Why no downside** (round 37): a willing soul walks in by choice, so Mortmain holds no influence over it. For the same reason it **speaks its own name** (see [Reading and echoes](../Mechanics/reading-and-echoes.md)).
- **Who made them** (round 17): only the few who knew the truth and chose stone anyway: the [Crimson Circle](../Organizations/the-crimson-circle.md) (round 35). Everyone else went to the rite expecting to be freed.

### Crimson

- **The sacrifice was willing, but an evil being is trapped inside,** so a Crimson rune always has a downside. **That downside comes from the prisoner, not from Mortmain's grip;** the jailer is willing. (round 37) See [Crimson](crimson.md).

### The wire (round 10)

- **The player can see it: forced stones are bound with iron wire; willing stones are bare.**
- **The wire was drawn from the dead man's own mail.** The rite bound him in what he wore. Gleaners did the wiring (round 28).
- **Iron is the jailer's metal,** a truth only the Wanderer comes to learn; the common people don't know runes hold souls. (round 10; narrowed round 22)
- **Why iron is refused as money** (round 22; replaces "the jailer's metal" as the folk reason): a glut (fields covered in mail, blades and helms) and wire money (iron in a purse means a cheat). Villagers still use scrap iron for nails and hinges. See [Currency](../Mechanics/currency-and-barter.md).
- **The irony** (round 22): people hate iron for cheating them, and the wire on a forced stone is the same cheat done to the dead.
- **Art** (ART_RULES §5): the wire is a world color (iron/ash greys), never a tier color; it must not cross the glyph or the notch edge; must read at 16px and 2x. One placement: two or three turns around one end, notches on the opposite edge.
- **On offering, the wire falls loose.** (round 12)
- **The wire's tail ties the stone on** (round 39): a forced stone is lashed to steel with its own wire tail. A willing stone, bare, needs cord, gut or thong. See [What runes are](what-runes-are.md).

## Open

- **Is the wire ever removed?** E.g. a loose soul the Wanderer binds and frees: wired, since binding is trapping. A willing volunteer bound into a stone: bare? Round 37 suggests bare (Mortmain has no grip on the willing), but binding is still trapping and adds corruption. **Flagged, not resolved.**

## Rejected

- **Deceived consent counts as willing.** Rejected round 8: souls taken under false rites are forced.
- **Willing runes have a downside too.** Rejected round 9.
- **No one trades iron because it is the jailer's metal** (round 10 reason). Replaced round 22: the people don't know, so it can't be their reason.
