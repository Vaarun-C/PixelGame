# Blanks: How Stones Are Made

A soul goes into stone only when two things meet: a blank stone made by a keeper, and the cut-short rite. Both are [Mortmain](../Gods/mortmain.md)'s craft. Anyone can say the words, but without a blank the soul just goes loose. Like a lock only one smith can forge: anyone can turn the key, nobody else can make the lock.

## Canon

- **The secret is the blank, not the words** (round 36).
- **Both halves are Mortmain's craft** (round 36):
  - **The blank:** a stone made by a keeper.
  - **The withholding rite:** the field form, the rite cut short before the release. The monk speaks the true name, as the rite requires, and leaves the words of release unsaid (round 19: the false rite withholds the release, not the name).
  - **A soul goes into stone only with both.** Without a blank, a cut-short rite leaves the soul loose (more Hollows).
- **Who can make blanks:** making stones always needed a keeper; no one could work it out from outside (round 34, user's rule). **Round 37 adds:** a few of the king's readers came from the Order and made the runes. See Open for how this squares with "only keepers".
- **In the war, keepers handed the chaplains blanks** dressed as keepsakes or prayer stones for the dying man to grip. The chaplain said the field form, unknowing. **"A shriving hand holds no stone" still holds:** the dying man held it. (round 36)
- **Gleaners** then pried the stone from the hand, wired it with the man's own mail and loaded the carts. (round 28)
- **The Paymaster's stones** came from blanks the keepers sold him. (round 36)
- **Blanks were the chaplain's tools, like a cook's ingredients** (round 38b; corrects round 38). The crown or company paid the keepers for blanks and handed them to the chaplains along with their wages, the same way the king had paid the keepers. **The captains believed they were prayer stones,** an ordinary part of the rite.
- **After the crowns' silver ran out (~11 years ago)** the keepers kept handing out blanks for almost nothing, because they needed the trapping, not the metal: **every trapping feeds Mortmain on its own.** **Supply never ran out** (round 38). The Wanderer's company kept handing him blanks, and he trapped souls until the last day.
- **Finished stones then had no buyer.** They piled up in chapel naves and lay where they fell on the fields. **That is why the world is full of runes to scavenge.** (round 38b; fits: runes as loot across every biome)
- **Stones were not company pay** (round 38b): wages stayed coin. The Paymaster is the exception (see [The Unfallen](../Characters/the-unfallen.md)).
- **Using leaked; making stayed secret** (round 36). Rivals took a few stones on seized blades and learned to read them by doing what they name, but had no way to make more. That is why every crown bought the moment the keepers began selling.
- **The Crimson Circle knew the craft:** its founders were the king's readers, some from the Order, who made the runes for him. (round 37; see [The Crimson Circle](../Organizations/the-crimson-circle.md))
- **A rebuilt rune is still the old blank underneath,** patched with mortar by a carver. (round 37; see [Rebuilding](rebuilding-and-binding.md))
- **Trapping feeds Mortmain.** (round 37)

## Open

- **"Only keepers" vs the Circle's runemakers** (round 37 proposal a, not decided): the Order brothers among the king's readers were honest monks the keepers taught, believing it a holy craft. Then round 34's "making always took a keeper" becomes "only the keepers and the few they taught".
- **Who trapped the beast souls** (round 37 proposal b, conflicts with canon): round 34 canon says the keepers trapped the beasts and the readers only set the stones. The user's round 37 story says the readers put the souls inside. Proposal: the readers trapped the beasts themselves, so they fed Mortmain unknowingly for years and their jailing is atonement. Proposed jailer echo: "We put the ox in the plough. We thought it was only an ox." **Needs the user to pick one.**
- **Does a willing stone need a blank,** or only the prisoner's half of a Crimson seal? (round 36)

## Rejected

- **The false rite held the name back** (round 18 wording). Replaced round 19: it withholds the release; the name is spoken, so the Wanderer's guilt stays real and fits "he doesn't remember the names he spoke".
- **Hush takes the soul into the stone** (round 19). Replaced round 36: the holding is Mortmain's.
- **Blanks running short** (round 36 proposal: late-war rites with no blank loosed souls, so the dead outran the living). Rejected round 38: supply had no reason to run out, since the captains carrying on fed the Quiet gods. "The dead outran the living" keeps its cause: stones broken unspoken.
- **Keepers sold blanks to captains and stones became company pay** (round 38). Replaced round 38b: the company bought blanks as chaplains' tools, wages stayed coin, and after the silver ran out keepers gave blanks for almost nothing.
