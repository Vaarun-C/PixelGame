# Origin of Runes: The False Last Rites

People went to the monks to be freed at death. Some monks trapped them in stones instead. The Order taught that a stone was only a harmless relic, so people kept trusting the rite while stones became weapons and money.

## Canon

### The false rites (round 7; replaces the round 4 vow)

- **People went to the monks for last rites,** believing the monks would free their souls to the good gods.
- **The monks trapped them instead.** The souls went into stones; the stones were kept in chapel reliquaries. (fits monk: "Every stone out there was once a promise.")
- **These souls count as forced.** They agreed to a rite, not to a stone. (round 8)
- **How it works:** a keeper-made blank plus the withholding rite, both Mortmain's craft. See [Blanks](blanks.md). (rounds 19, 36)
- **The corrupted monks kept giving last rites** with one part changed, so it spread unseen; the reliquaries filled. (rounds 6–7, 18)
- **The dead came back hollow,** because their souls were somewhere else. (kept from round 4)

### The relic doctrine (round 17)

- **The Order taught that a stone is a harmless relic,** what a name leaves behind once the soul has gone free. So people spent stones and still trusted the rite. The truth (the soul is in the stone) is what the Wanderer uncovers. How it survived reading: see [Reading and echoes](../Mechanics/reading-and-echoes.md) (round 25).
- **It was believed because a stone looked like the keepsake** every family kept (Mortmain's keepsake, round 18).

### Wielding, trade and the war

- **The kingdoms learned stones could be wielded; the war turned into a harvest of names.** (kept from round 4) Stones were first wielded in the testament king's own reign (round 34); other crowns got them only when the keepers sold to everyone (round 35). See [The testament king](../Characters/the-testament-king.md).
- **Runes became a currency** once the kingdoms learned to wield them. (round 16) **Of crowns, not of soldiers** (round 38b): the crowns bought finished stones with silver as weapons, war chests and ransoms. Wages stayed coin.
- **How rune-currency ended** (round 38b): the crowns' silver ran out (~11 years ago) and finished stones had no buyer. The keepers kept handing out blanks for almost nothing, because every trapping feeds Mortmain on its own; the captains took them for prayer stones. Unsold stones piled up in chapel naves and lay where they fell, which is why the world is full of runes. **The Paymaster is the exception,** a captain who paid his men in stones. See [Blanks](blanks.md).
- **The rune trade drove more forced trapping:** souls were taken for profit, which raised corruption and made the dark gods stronger. (round 16)
- **War chaplains carried the rite to the battlefields,** so the harvest was the rite done at scale. Some gave it without knowing what it did; the Wanderer was one. (round 9) Who knew and the chain of stones: see [The Order](../Organizations/the-order.md) (round 28).
- **Why the dead rise** (round 10): in the war, stones were broken unspoken by the thousand (captured stones smashed to deny the enemy, stones cracked under hooves and carts, satchels burst in the mud). Each loosed a soul; a loose soul crawls into an empty body and walks. That is a Hollow. See [Hollows](../Characters/hollows.md).
- **Crimson prisons were the last answer to what the war woke.** (kept from round 4; the Crimson Circle began in the king's border wars, round 37)

## Open

- **When the relic doctrine began** (history review round 36, point 9). The king's champions read prisoners' stones and heard real echoes before any doctrine existed, and stones sat in reliquaries ~80 years before him. Proposal: the Order always called reliquary stones relics, but wrote the doctrine down and preached it only when it began selling to every crown. The king's men who knew wasted, died, or became the Unfallen. (Round 37 adds: the king's inner circle of readers learned the truth, so at least they knew.)
- **What the stones were for before the king** (history point 12): reading taken, Hush's larder (the reliquaries' first use).
- **How a market in stones squared with believing the rite freed people:** the relic doctrine (round 17); see Reading and echoes for the remaining proposals.
- **Are runes still traded?** (round 16 hole 3) Barterers take only metal, but the pitch has players trading runes. Proposals: the market for war-stones died with the armies; villagers half-know the stones are the dead, so a barterer handles one like grave goods; stones still change hands person to person. **IP flag:** runes as currency is Elden Ring's by name; souls as currency is Dark Souls'. Keeping rune-money in the past and metal as the money of play keeps clear.
- **Withheld:** whether the man who wrote the relic doctrine believed it. (round 25, 26)

## Rejected

- **Rune origins: names as the old treasury; a war against an unbeatable thing; names falling out of a dying world** (round 3 options 2–4). Rejected round 4 for the Chapels' vow.
- **The Chapels' vow:** soldiers gave their names to reliquaries believing a name in stone outlives death (canon round 4). Replaced round 7: it is last rites; the betrayal is the monks', not the soldiers' bargain.
- **Stones as company pay in the war's last ten years** (round 38) and **the currency sinking from crowns to companies** (round 16 proposal). Replaced round 38b: wages stayed coin (wire money broke pay), and the currency ended when the crowns' silver ran out.
- **True names replacing runes; the player as a wizard; the Wanderer as an enemy or boss** (round 1). Rejected round 2: runes are the core of the pitch, the economy and the art rules; melee and the HD sprites are built; fixed names would be spoiled by wikis. The useful parts became reading, utterances and names of kinds.
