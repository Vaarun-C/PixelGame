# More Root Names (proposals)

The user wants more rune types than Rend, Ward and Hunger. The rule and the eight built names below are canon; the rest is the candidate list from round 21.

## Canon

- **A soul's root name is the verb strongest in the man at his death** (round 21 proposal, settled round 41 with the [rune families](rune-types.md)): it is the subtype; the family is what he was doing as he died.

- **Eight root names are built** (round 42, Godot build): Rend, Break, Sting (Grudge); Ward, Still, Sink (Mortmain); Hunger, Flee (Hush). See [Rune types](rune-types.md). Watch and the rest below stay candidates.

## Open

- **Root name vs soldier** (round 21): the rule is now canon (above). Still open: "most often how he died or what he did most"; examples, a man who died running gives Flee, one trampled gives Break.
- **Cost in the game:** runes.js has three types; `FITS` puts Rend and Hunger in the blade and Ward in the armor; the icon sheet is rows = type, cols = tier (ART_RULES §13). Each new type needs a glyph row (6 tiers, readable at 16px), passives, fuse order, resonances and biome odds. Battlefield and Frozen Pass favour no type today (biomes.js).
- **Rules for candidates:** physical verbs a body or beast does, never elements. **No fire root name** (fire is Gled's and Grudge's; "what burns is free of the hand"). No forgetting (Hush's). No status-list names (bleed, frost, poison, rot, madness: Elden Ring's set).
- **Candidates:**
  - **Break:** breaking (a hoof on bone, frost splitting rock, a ram on a gate, a falling tree). Glyph: a straight stroke snapped, halves offset. Blade. Hits build stagger; a full build breaks a Hollow's thrust. Battlefield. Souls often trampled past recognising, so a **nameless** need is common.
  - **Flee:** running from death (a hare, a deer, a routed company, a deserter). Glyph: a stroke with a back-bent tail, like a track in snow. Armor. Longer or quicker dodge, faster run. Frozen Pass (proposal: the pass was the deserters' road; the ice tombs hold men who ran). Souls often ashamed, so **letting go** is common ("Don't let them see what I did.").
  - **Sting:** hurting what touches you (nettle, wasp, a cornered rat, a caltrop, the impaling stakes). Glyph: a short stroke with a barb turned back. Armor. Attackers take back a share of the blow. Battlefield second, or Ashen Forest.
  - **Sink:** being dragged down (mud, deep water, mail on a drowning man, the dead piled on the living). Glyph: a stroke bowed under a bar. Blade. Struck foes slow for a moment. Rot Swamp second.
  - **Still:** stopping (a held breath, a snare drawn tight, a corpse stiffening). Glyph: a short bar pinning a stroke. Blade. A struck Hollow's wind-up is held a beat. Frozen Pass second. Risk: reads as an ice school; keep the text on breath and stiffness, never cold.
  - **Watch:** watching (a crow on a gibbet, a sentry, a wolf at the treeline). Glyph: an open ring with one stroke. Needs a third lashing place (helm); not for now. Runestones and Hollows show earlier at the screen edge. Never a lie detector (that is Ennick's).
  - **Knit:** closing (a scab, bark over a cut, bone setting crooked). Armor. Slow healing. Overlaps Hunger and Scab; weakest candidate.
- **Names of kinds grow with them:** a bear is Break and Hunger; a hare Flee and Watch; a wasp Sting and Flee.
- **Recommended first batch: Break (blade) and Flee (armor).** They fill the two biomes with no favoured type, balance the lashing spots, give stagger and mobility, and tie type to need. Sting third if armor needs damage. Example resonances: Rend + Break "Hew" (heavy hits bleed and stagger); Break x3 "Sunder"; Flee + Ward "Give Ground" (after a dodge, the next blow lands lighter); Flee x2 "Rout" (a dodge through a Hollow staggers it).
