# The Order

The monks' order, now broken. Almost everyone in it believed the rite freed souls: village monks, war chaplains, gleaners, and the clerks who graded and sold stones at the Mother House. Only the hidden keepers knew. Think of a bakery where only a few bakers know the flour is poisoned.

## Canon

- **The order is broken.** No inquisitors, no ruling church; what's left is monks in village walls. (round 4)
- **Some monks have gone over to the dark gods; which ones is hidden.** They pass as ordinary tired monks; the player learns slowly, through persistent villages and the story. (rounds 7–8) So "ashamed" is how every monk looks; for some it is true, for others cover. (round 7, replacing round 4's "ashamed, not villainous")
- **Why they still hide, now their gods are strong:** the trap only works on those who trust the rite. A monk known to serve the dark gods gets no dying at his door. (round 9)
- **The rite fee keeps them** (round 23): a village monk lives on what families pay for the last rite; it keeps only himself and his chapel. **Monks have no families.**
- **The cheap monk is how the dark monks got in** (round 23): they undercut, and poor families sometimes got the false rite. **But dear monks can be corrupt too, so price is no tell.** In the war the crowns paid for the rite and the chaplains gave it free by the thousand; that free rite was the false one. No one living knows this.
- **Carvers are the monks.** (round 5)

### Who knew: the layers (round 28)

- **The abbot of the Mother House.** Took the crowns' silver and didn't ask. **Whether he knew is a mystery,** kept for a late reveal.
- **The Mother House brothers:** clerks, graders and sellers. Graded stones, kept the master ledgers, sold to the crowns. Certified the king's echo as his will (round 27). Believed the doctrine.
- **Village monks:** gave the full rite and believed.
- **War chaplains:** gave the **field form**, the rite "shortened for haste": say the name, tie the cord, move on. The words left out were the release. They believed, and never held a stone. (The Wanderer was one.) Keepers handed them blanks dressed as keepsakes or prayer stones for the dying to grip (round 36); the crown or company paid the keepers and passed the blanks to the chaplains with their coin wages (round 38b). **They learned both forms** (round 38): the long rite, and the field form, taught as the war's hasty dispensation.
- **Gleaners:** lay servants. Followed the chaplains with pliers and satchels, wired each stone with its man's own mail and loaded the carts. Never lashed one to steel, so never read one.
- **The [keepers](keepers.md):** few, hidden at every level. **The only ones who knew.** They wrote the field form, made the blanks, ran the trade and killed the stone-sick.
- **The chain:** chaplain → gleaner → cart → reliquary → Mother House ledger → crown.
- **Round 37 note:** a few of the king's readers came from the Order and made runes; they learned the truth and became the [Crimson Circle](the-crimson-circle.md). Reading taken: "the only ones who knew" means the only ones still inside the Order's chain.

## Open

- **The order sold to every crown,** so one side's dead were spent against their own sons; Mortmain didn't light the war, his keepers kept it fed. (round 26 proposal)
- **Crown quartermasters bought by glow** and believed the doctrine because it suited them; captains lashed and read. (round 28 proposal)
- **What shows in game:** ledger sale pages with cord number, grade, buyer and price in marks; the Battlefield's abandoned carts and crates as gleaners' carts (wire coils, pliers, burst satchels); villager lines about sides and shut gates.
- **Withheld:** whether the abbot knew (late reveal; his fate is tied to it).

## Rejected

- **"The monks are ashamed, not villainous"** (round 4). Replaced round 7.
- **Married lay-brother monks** (round 23). Rejected.
- **The keepers started the war** (round 26 alternative). Not chosen: makes them all-powerful; better that they only fed it.
