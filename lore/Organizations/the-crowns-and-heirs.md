# The Crowns and the Heirs

The testament king's two sons split his kingdom over "Not him". Both died before the war did; their claims passed to sons, regents and captains, and the fight became about the sentence, not the men. Several small neighbouring crowns joined in for land, switching sides as their fortunes turned or they were bought. Nobody won. The crowns weren't destroyed; they were forgotten.

## Canon (rounds 26–27)

- **The heirs started the war, splitting the kingdom over "Not him."** Each swore it meant the other.
- **The heirs are the king's sons, and there are two of them** (round 38d; seen in: prologue line 2, "His sons each swore it meant the other."). Two heirs, not two crowns: the neighbours keep the war many-sided.
- **The claims outlived the sons** (round 38e): when a son died, his claim passed on, to his own sons, to a regent, or to captains fighting in his name. Both sons were dead before the war ended; the war outlived them and became about the sentence more than the men. Each new claimant swore "Not him" meant the other line.
- **Several small neighbouring crowns, all unnamed,** backed one claimant or another for land, and switched sides as heirs died or were bought. (rounds 26–27; "heirs" here means whoever held a son's claim at the time, round 38e)
- **Soldiers fought** for the heir they believed the king meant, or more often for whoever paid, and changed sides when the pay stopped. (fits the proposed pikeman line, "Ours. Then theirs, when ours stopped paying.")
- **No crown won.** The war ran out (see [The testament war](../Timeline/the-testament-war.md)).
- **The crowns' fate: forgotten.** The war lasted a generation and no single event destroyed them; they faded out of memory (the Greying), as the coin faces did.
- **The crowns got stones only when the keepers sold to everyone,** and bought the moment they could, because they had a few seized stones and no way to make more. (rounds 35–36)
- **They paid the war in coin until it broke:** debased silver, then wire money. (round 22) Crown vaults and war chests are fallen.

## Open

- **Forgotten inside living memory** (history review round 36, point 5): the crowns faded only ~3 years after the war, while veterans who took their pay still live. The Greying's only stated cause is offering. The heirs' fate isn't stated at all. Fixes: (a) recommended: **the keepers offered the great stones as the crowns fell:** each dead king's and heir's stone was the richest in its realm, so their names went to Hush one by one. (b) People refuse to say the names (weakens the Greying).
- **What became of the two sons:** settled only that both died before the war ended (round 38e). When, how, and whether their souls were stoned (and so offered or forgotten) are unstated; ties into question 4 (how the heirs were forgotten).
- **Who carried each claim, and how many times it changed hands,** is unstated.
- **Did each new claimant need a new reading of the king?** Every claimant needed his own sworn reader (round 27), so each handover may have meant another reading and more prisoners cut, until the stone was offered. Not settled.
- **Round 16 hole 7, who fought whom:** settled round 26–27 in outline; the order selling to every crown is still a proposal (see [The Order](the-order.md)).

## Rejected

- **Both sons lived through the whole war** (round 38d option a). Not chosen round 38e: the claim passing down fits "switched sides as heirs died" and makes the war about the sentence.
- **Two great crowns.** Too close to Berserk's long two-kingdom war; gives the war a clean shape it shouldn't have. (round 26)
- **A hollow victor crown whose fortress still stands.** Weakens R1 (no mint). (round 26)
- **The crowns sacked the chapels for their stones** (round 26 ending reason 3). Not chosen.
- **W1, a plain succession war over the king's stone as regalia** (liked, too generic; kept only as the testament's base); **W2, hungry years as the cause** (kept as background); **W3, the first wielding, an arms race** (generic; clashes with "the war turned into a harvest"). (round 27)
