# The Crimson Circle

The few who knew what stones really were and chose stone anyway. They began as the testament king's inner circle of readers, learned the truth when he started making stones of prisoners, and turned the craft against his strongest, maddened guards. Most gave themselves as jailers; the handful left are taken for madmen.

## Canon

- **Origin: the king's inner circle of readers** (round 37, the user's story). A few came from the Order and made the runes; the others put the souls inside (the first tool runes, the beast stones). The king knew of the Quiet gods, so he didn't question how it worked.
- **They learned the truth** when the king began making runes from prisoners of war: the truth about the Quiet gods and the real rites. (round 37)
- **They then turned the craft to imprison the strongest of the king's guards,** who had been driven mad but were immensely powerful from the strong runes they used. **So the sealing began in the border wars,** before the testament war. (round 37)
- **They knew the craft,** so they could make stones (blanks included) without the keepers. (round 37; settles the round 35 "how did the circle make stones" and round 36 "where did it get blanks")
- **They are the only makers of willing runes:** the few who knew the truth and chose stone anyway. (round 17) **Willing stones speak their own name;** the Circle were the ones who noticed. (round 25)
- **Name: the Crimson Circle** (round 35). When it took the name is open (no Crimson stone existed before the first sealing).
- **Why it stayed silent** (round 35): **many of its first members gave themselves up as jailers, sealing some of the strongest warriors of the king's army in Crimson. Only a handful are left, and their words are treated as ramblings.**
- **They sealed the [Unfallen](../Characters/the-unfallen.md)** with willing jailers, two names, one yes, and tore the prisoners' names into pieces across ledgers. (round 30) The six jailers all count as members (round 35 reading).
- **They wouldn't use a dark rite** (round 30), which is why each seal needed a willing jailer.

## Open

- **Knew "Not him" from the first day** (round 34 proposal): the readers knew the king served the Quiet gods through his keepers, so they knew what "Not him" meant. The king's old servant, the witness (jailer of the court keeper), fits as one of them.
- **The 20-year silence before the champion's sealing** (round 35 hole): if they knew "Not him", why wait? The round 34 fix: speaking meant being "sent to the chaplains", and no one would take their word over a certified will. They hid, kept ledgers and acted only when Unfallen appeared. Complicit by silence, which suits the tone. (Round 37 softens it: they were already sealing in the border wars.)
- **Proposal (a), round 37:** the Order brothers who made the runes were honest monks the keepers taught, believing it holy; "making always took a keeper" becomes "only the keepers and the few they taught".
- **Proposal (b), round 37:** the readers trapped the beast souls themselves, so they fed Mortmain unknowingly for years and their jailing is atonement. **Conflicts with round 34** (keepers trapped the beasts; readers only set them). Jailer echo (proposed): "We put the ox in the plough. We thought it was only an ox."
- **Clean hands** (round 34): they were the king's tools first; worth one jailer echo that admits it.
- **Where they met; the milestone mark** beside "Not him": the widow's secret (round 31).
- **Survivors as NPCs:** see [Villagers](../Characters/villagers.md).
- **Reading A** (withering and famine before the king's death) is still unconfirmed; see [The testament king](../Characters/the-testament-king.md).
